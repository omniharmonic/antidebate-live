## 1. Verification of the three "published synthesis" claims

**Main finding.** The Synthesis Media write-up (the "published synthesis" proper) lists none of the three items as its points of synthesis. It gives three different points: shared concern about concentrated power, alignment as the central problem, and the crux of AI catastrophe risk vs government overreach (see §2).

The three items in `prior-debates.md` look like they came mainly from Jasmine Li's audience notes. Items 1 and 2 are strongly supported there and in the recording. Item 3 is not.

### 1. "Building frontier-lab auditing and verification systems": **SUPPORTED**
- JL (TLDR): "They both agree on: importance of 3rd-party verification and auditing of labs". JL (agreement list): "Two logical next steps they both endorse: Verification and auditing of labs."
- SM (partial): auditing is mentioned only as a qualifier inside the alignment point: "neither markets nor governments can be blindly trusted to handle it well without better verification, auditing, and incentives."
- REC, about **01:22:45–01:22:54**. Speaker inferred to be Ball, responding to Kokotajlo's closing. He was the one who attended the India AI summit a few lines earlier. "the two logical next things to do are work on whatever this thing is going to be for like the verification, the auditing of the Frontier Labs, whatever."
- REC, about **01:23:57–01:24:04**, Boeree's summary: "you also both agree that … AI alignment itself continues to be a major concern and that we should be working on auditing and verification methods of Frontier Labs".
- Origin in the debate: Ball proposed "independent third party auditors" (REC about 00:24:17 and 00:30:13).
- Caveat: Kokotajlo was sceptical that auditors would matter in time (JL: "He has trouble believing third-party auditors will succeed fast enough"; REC about 00:30:45). The convergence formed late.

### 2. "Establishing preconditions for a US–China bilateral agreement": **SUPPORTED**
- JL: "Conditions for a US–China bilateral agreement should be established — even if the deal itself is far off." Also: "Establish conditions so that the US and China could bilaterally agree to slow down or shut things down."
- SM: **not mentioned**.
- REC, about **00:44:29–00:44:40** (Kokotajlo, after Ball's parity question): "I've come to think that actually the top priority is to make sure that the preconditions of a deal are are met. And one of those preconditions is that the governments of the respective … countries know where the chips are".
- REC, about **01:23:00–01:23:10** (inferred Ball): "try to establish the conditions where perhaps at some point in the future the US and China could bilaterally agree to shut things down."
- REC, about **01:24:04–01:24:12** (Boeree): "so that we can try and establish conditions where US and China can have some kind of bilateral agreement".
- Caveat: Ball stresses that it will be slow ("the ball moves very slowly", about 01:22:01) and that he worries "about the bilateral much less the … multilateral" (about 01:22:36).

### 3. "Transparency requirements as the immediate step": **PARTIALLY SUPPORTED (weak). Not a stated convergence.**
- SM: **not mentioned**. JL: **not in the agreement list**. JL attributes transparency to Kokotajlo alone: "preferred policy ideas: Advocate for transparency" and "Advocates for transparency regulations."
- REC, about **00:37:31–00:37:37**. Kokotajlo answers Ball's clarifying question before the steel-man: "Right now in the current administration, given the current situation, I advocate for transparency stuff along the lines of the things we've talked about." This is his *own* near-term ask, not a joint one.
- REC, about **01:09:36–01:09:40** (Kokotajlo): "this is part of why I think Dean and I have both been advocating for transparency regulations". This is a claim about their prior shared advocacy. TB also notes they "co-authored a TIME piece on AI transparency". The captions show Ball did not assert it himself.
- Neither participant nor the moderator states transparency as the agreed immediate step. The jointly stated "logical next things" are items 1 and 2.
- Recommendation: drop it from the gold synthesis. At most, keep it as a context note ("pre-existing shared position asserted by Kokotajlo").

### Disagreements that remained

- **Core stance.** Kokotajlo: regulation is necessary because of concentration of power and loss of control. Ball: government intervention will likely make things worse, and he prefers "market incentives, third-party auditors, and new private institutions" (JL TLDR).
- **Alignment by markets.** "Ball thinks alignment will be solved by the market. Kokotajlo is much less confident." (JL.) Ball: "I just feel like markets are going to channel this better … I could be wrong." (REC about 00:51:35.)
- **Next steps and the future.** "They disagree on logical next steps and on what 2030 looks like" (JL). In the closing, Ball puts the disagreement in what the future looks like rather than the next step: "we actually might kind of agree on like what the logical next step is and … where we disagree much more profoundly is like what is going to…" (REC about 01:18:58–01:19:10).
- **Anthropic vs the Department of War.** Kokotajlo predicts Anthropic loses. Ball "Thinks Anthropic made the right move" and thinks it could win a lawsuit (JL; REC cold open about 00:00:23).
- **Domestic vs international.** Kokotajlo argues the next step includes designing the international agreement (REC about 01:20:56–01:21:11). Ball stresses how slowly international processes move (about 01:21:58).
