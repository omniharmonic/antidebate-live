## 1. What it is

- "a new and open-source format for debate where participants build on each other's insights, so that greater complexity can emerge." (both guides; the Synthesis Media post)
- The format has three phases (both guides, §1):
  - "Phase 1: Clarifying Difference — crystallizing our different 'ingredients'"
  - "Phase 2: Exploring Synthesis — exploring how we might mix our [different] ingredients into something new"
  - "Phase 3: Taking Stock — asking ourselves: how did my view expand, if at all?"
- The landing page phrases it as: Phase 1 is "differentiating the perspectives — with opening statements, rebuttals, and some time for unstructured debate", and Phase 2 is "exploring synthesis — through steel-manning, red-teaming, and open-ended synthesis-seeking."
- The goal: "the goal of the Anti-Debate is not to change minds, but to expand minds." Tagline: "a debate that everyone can win." (Synthesis Media post)
- It is "not anti-debate! It actually starts out like a traditional debate with opening statements and rebuttals, but then, it doesn't stop there." (Synthesis Media post)
- Lineage (Synthesis Media post): the name comes from William MacAskill's 2017 proposal of "anti-debates". It was popularised by Peter Limberger and Connor Barnes, and Perspectiva has run its own "antidebate" since 2022. Lepp drew on Socratic dialogue, Oxford debate and Bohmian dialogue, among others, and ran trial runs before the v1 launch.
- Discourse Matrix: practices fall into three groups: **debate** ("Competitive exchange of opposing viewpoints"), **dialogue** ("Sharing ideas to foster understanding and connection") and **dialectic** ("Collaborative inquiry for deeper truth and synthesis"). Synthesis Media's Anti-Debate is listed under dialectic. An Anti-Debate "may work best when previous debates on the issue haven't been generative and the goal is a more comprehensive view."

## 2. Ground Rules (anti-debate.org/ground-rules.html)

"Participants should review and agree to these principles before diving in":
1. **Engage in Good Faith**: "don't come to dunk. Come to expand your mind." This applies to the host too: "don't post clips out of context or misrepresent what happened."
2. **Practice Epistemic Humility**: "Update your beliefs in the face of new information."
3. **Abide by the Format**: "Stick to time limits and don't interrupt."
4. **Disclose Conflicts of Interest**: "Do you have financial, political, or otherwise personal interests in the topic?"

## 3. Prep (before the anti-debate)

**Participants**
- Debaters "should have significantly different perspectives on the topic, but be willing to play ball with the format, e.g. steel-man the opposing perspective" (short guide). A personal connection to the topic can help.
- Moderator: "with strong facilitation skills and ideally also domain expertise."
- *Long guide only*, optional roles: Judge(s) ("1+ individuals or crowdsourced from the audience"), Time-Keeper & Poll Administrator, and Fact-Checker (live or before airing; "A live fact-checker may not need to interrupt, e.g. if fact-checks come up on a screen"; "On X, Community Notes can be used for live fact-checking").

**Topic criteria**: Debated; Consequential; Synthesis sought; Specific ("it's easier to start specific and go broad (e.g. The Billionaire Tax), rather than start broad and grope for specifics (e.g. inequality)"). The question is framed as yes/no because it "helps debaters (and the audience) clearly gauge which side they're on, and how their views change." Speaker roles in the format are therefore **affirmative** and **negative**.

**Long guide only**
- Competitive or not: if competitive, you need judges and/or an audience. If not, "there could still be a verdict on whether the anti-debate was 'won,' i.e. met a threshold of success."
- Time-keeping tip: "hold it up when there are 30 seconds left, then 0 seconds left. Then cut the person off after they go 10 seconds over."
- A conversational setting ("sitting around a table") rather than a confrontational one.
- Two prep meetings: one with all participants, and a 1:1 with the moderator.

**Moderator prep** (both guides): script the introduction (what the format is, its 3 phases, framing of the topic, introducing the debaters); adapt the slides; "Keep a tight leash! Hold debaters to the format and Ground Rules."; "Try to avoid areas of agreement in phase 1, and save them for phase 2. At that point, help debaters identify points of synthesis! Synthesis is the payload."

Additional moderator guidance **in the long guide only**:
- Research the topic and have statistics on hand, "but be careful to not put your thumb on the scale."
- Optionally adapt a full moderator script.
- During the event:
  - "If steel-manning isn't done in good faith, call it out"
  - "If debaters speak too abstractly, ask for specific examples"
  - "If debaters drift, bring them back to the question"
  - Ask "what do you mean?"
  - "If debaters use jargon, ask them to define the term. If they can't agree on a definition, agree to not use the term for the rest of the anti-debate."
- An optional "3-strikes rule" for Ground Rule violations.
- "Follow along closely so you can periodically summarize areas of disagreement, areas of agreement, points of synthesis, etc."

**Debater prep** (both guides):
- Send each other 1–2 references (articles or podcasts).
- Optionally prepare a 3-minute personal story.
- Prepare a 6-minute opening statement, "ideally including specific examples".

The *long guide* adds "helpful questions to think through in advance":
- evidence that would change my position
- "Under what circumstances does my position not apply?"
- failure modes
- non-falsifiable elements
- points with "no adequate rebuttal"
- credence level
- what I agree with in the opposing position
- what it would take to resolve the disagreements, using the three integration questions below

## 4. Round structure

Both guides give the same ordered format for a "moderated anti-debate between two individuals in front of a live audience" (long guide wording). The short guide says it "should take ~1.5 hours". "Aff" means affirmative and "Neg" means negative. Durations are copied as stated. Items marked OPTIONAL are optional in the source.

### Phase 1: Clarifying Difference (~45 mins total, "00:00:00-00:45:00")

| # | Round | Who speaks (order) | Duration | Goal | Moderator does |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | **Introduction** | Moderator | 3–4 mins | Introduce the Anti-Debate | Delivers the scripted intro: what the format is and its 3 phases, framing of the topic, and introduces the debaters |
| 1.2 | **Pre-Poll** (OPTIONAL) | Audience | 1–2 mins | Baseline for later comparison ("Save the results for future comparison") | Puts the question on screen and runs three 1–5 scales: (1) views on the question (1 most negative, 5 most affirmative); (2) comprehensiveness of views; (3) faith in public discourse |
| 1.3 | **Connection Before Content** (OPTIONAL) | Neg, then Aff | 3 mins each | Personal story: "Why do you personally care about this issue?" | Not stated beyond sequencing |
| 1.4a | **Opening Statements** | Aff, then Neg | 6 mins each | Each debater states their position (prep: "ideally including specific examples") | Time-keeping. Not otherwise stated |
| 1.4b | **Rebuttals** | Aff, then Neg | 3 mins each | Rebut the opponent | Not stated |
| 1.5 | **Open Debate** | Both, unstructured | up to 10 mins | "Unstructured, open debate" | OPTIONAL: "introduces additional context, specific scenarios, and/or summarizes areas of disagreement" |

### Phase 2: Exploring Synthesis (~30 mins total, "45:00-01:15:00")

| # | Round | Who speaks (order) | Duration | Goal | Moderator does |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | **Steel-Manning Each Other**, part A (Neg steel-mans Aff) | Neg asks a clarifying question (1 min), Aff responds (1 min), **Neg steel-mans Aff** (3 mins), Aff may respond (1 min) | ~6 mins | "the debater switches sides, and presents the strongest version of their opponent's position" | Opens by asking Neg: "is there anything you need to know to accurately steel-man?" (long guide: call out steel-manning not done in good faith) |
| 2.1 | **Steel-Manning Each Other**, part B (Aff steel-mans Neg) | Aff asks a clarifying question (1 min), Neg responds (1 min), **Aff steel-mans** (3 mins), Neg may respond (1 min) | ~6 mins | Same as part A | Asks Aff the same opening question. Both guides literally say "Affirmative steel-mans affirmative's position". **This is almost certainly a typo for "negative's position"**; the parallel structure implies it. |
| 2.2 | **Red-Teaming Ourselves** | Aff (3 mins), then Neg may respond (1 min); then Neg (3 mins), then Aff may respond (1 min) | ~8 mins | Each answers "what would it take to change my position? The more specific, the better" (short: "e.g. specific statistics"; long: "e.g. including actual evidence") | Not stated beyond sequencing |
| 2.3 | **Exploring Integration** (short guide: "this is the heart of the anti-debate!") | Moderator, then both debaters in open dialogue | up to 12 mins | "debaters engage in unstructured, open dialogue to find points of synthesis" | First reads aloud: (1) "Is there an either/or that can be flipped to a both/and?" (2) "Is there an opportunity to shift from 'yes or no' to 'under what circumstances (if any)'?" (3) "Are there perverse incentives that are making the issue harder to resolve?" OPTIONAL: "summarizes areas of agreement and/or points of synthesis". OPTIONAL: invites debaters and/or audience to co-create something, from a "synthesis statement" up to "a proposed policy or product" |
| 2.4 | **Audience Participation** (OPTIONAL) | Audience | up to 5 mins | Audience contributes points of synthesis. "Please keep responses to points of synthesis; general Q&A is at the end." | Solicits the contributions and holds them to synthesis |

### Phase 3: Taking Stock (~15 mins, "1:15:00-1:30:00")

| # | Round | Who speaks (order) | Duration | Goal | Moderator does |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | **Personal Check-In** (OPTIONAL) | Aff, then Neg | 1 min each | "how do you feel right now? Identify any emotions and sensations in the body" | Poses the question |
| 3.2 | **Contemplation** (OPTIONAL) | Everyone, silent | short: 1–2 mins; long: 1 min | Reflect "on whether or how their view has expanded, if at all"; debaters prepare closings | Not stated |
| 3.3 | **Closing Statements** | Neg, then Aff | 4 mins each | Answer "how has your position expanded, if at all?" | Not stated |
| 3.4 | **Post-Poll** (OPTIONAL) | Audience | 1–2 mins | Same three questions as the pre-poll | Administers it |
| 3.5 | **Outro** | Moderator | 4–5 mins | Close | "summarizes what was learned, thanks the debaters, and brings the anti-debate to a close". Compares pre- and post-poll results if a poll was run. Points people to anti-debate.org |
| 3.6 | **Audience Q&A** | Audience | not stated | "audience reflections and questions" | Consider asking "what worked and didn't? What would you change about the format?" |

Observations relevant to the build. These are inferences, not source claims:
- Speaking order alternates by round: Aff goes first in openings, rebuttals, red-teaming and check-in; Neg goes first in connection, steel-manning and closings.
- The sum of stated Phase 1 durations (about 36–40 minutes including optional items) is below the stated "~45 mins". The slack is not accounted for.
- The phase windows are nominal. The sources say to "Adapt as necessary" (long guide).
- A **steel-man** round is structurally a speaker voicing the *opponent's* position. This matches ONTOLOGY §8.5: the speech act must not commit the speaker.
- A **red-teaming** round yields explicit update conditions ("what would it take to change my position"). These are crux-like material.

### Differences between the shorter and longer guides

The round sequence and durations are **identical**, with one exception: Contemplation is "1-2 mins" in the short guide and "1 min" in the long one. Other differences:

| Aspect | Short | Long |
|---|---|---|
| Audience | "experienced facilitators" | "people who aren't experienced at facilitation" |
| Optional roles (judge, time-keeper/poll admin, fact-checker) | absent | present |
| Competitive vs non-competitive decision, judging criteria, tie rules | absent | present |
| Logistics (setting, phone time-keeping) | absent | present |
| Prep meetings | one prep meeting | two (all participants + moderator 1:1) |
| In-the-moment moderator tactics (call out bad-faith steel-man, ask for examples, jargon rule, 3-strikes, periodic summaries) | only "Keep a tight leash!" and "save agreement for phase 2" | full list |

| Red-teaming example | "specific statistics" | "including actual evidence" |
| Exploring Integration label | "this is the heart of the anti-debate!" | no such label |

## 5. After the anti-debate (both guides)
- Debrief with debaters and/or audience ("what worked and didn't?"). Optionally use a feedback form.
- "Consider fact-checking your anti-debate before it airs".
- Title with "x", not "vs." (e.g. "ANTI-DEBATE: Gender-Affirming Care ft. Layman Pascal x Shreef Bishay").
- Release with the hashtag #anti-debate and cc Stephanie Lepp or Synthesis Media, or share it privately with her.

## 6. Additional possibilities (long guide only)
- **Change the Question**: at the end of Phase 2, let debaters reframe the yes/no question using stems like "Under what circumstances (if any)...?", "To what extent…?", "How might we characterize the value of…?" and "What are the criteria for…?"
- **Synthesis Artifact**: at the end of Phase 2, co-create something, e.g. a "Steel Venn or Integral Matrix". Handouts are possible so the audience can take part.
- **Free Association**, **Empathy Circles**, **Somatic Narration** (e.g. "as I hear you say that, I feel a tightening in my chest…"), **Narration for the Tribe** (voice one's memetic tribe) and **Socratic Search Space** (Vervaeke). Here the audience contributes questions and helps "identify synthesis possibilities the participants might miss".
